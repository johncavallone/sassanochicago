Which families married which.

Cousin marriage told us how closely related couples tended to be. This is a different question: regardless of blood relation, which surnames kept choosing each other, generation after generation?

Two surnames sit at the center of nearly everything: Calandriello and d'Amato. Between them they appear in a huge share of the tree's marriages — Calandriello alone shows up in 549 marriages across 106 different partner surnames, d'Amato in 528 across 119. Calandriello and Petrizzo rise to 29 marriages, still the single most common alliance documented anywhere in the tree, and Calandriello and d'Amato rise to 27 in second. This update added 50 marriages and 17 pairings. Calandriello and Rubino hold at 22 in third and d'Amato and Petrizzo at 20. The movement is further down: Calandriello and Trotta, Calandriello and Ferro, and d'Alessio and di Brizzi each add one to reach 13, Petrizzo and Trotta reach 11, and d'Amato and di Lisa, new to the table, also reach 11. Petrizzo is the biggest gainer among individual surnames, adding eight marriages (to 448); Calandriello adds five (549), Trotta and di Brizzi four each (312 and 262), d'Amato, d'Alessio and Ferro three each (528, 340 and 215), Feminella, di Bella and Pellegrino two each (328, 249 and 233), and Rubino, di Miele, di Luca, Libretti and Stavola one each (277, 241, 212, 209 and 192); Russo, Carlo, Pinto, Arnone and Florendino hold at 285, 240, 238, 215 and 192. No surname changes rank, though Ferro draws level with Arnone on 215 and Stavola with Florendino on 192. The same-surname count rises to 103, with Cammarano reaching 5 (the new case is the first-cousin marriage of Giacomo Cammarano and Maria Rosa Cammarano, one of the cousin marriages added this update; see the Sassano Marriages page), while Calandriello holds on 17, d'Amato on 13, Petrizzo on 8 and di Brizzi on 6. Petrizzo, d'Alessio, di Brizzi and di Luca each gain a new partner surname (105, 97, 81 and 80). (Last update added 32 marriages and 13 pairings, and d'Alessio and di Brizzi, and Calandriello and Zozzaro, each reached 12; the update before that added 31 marriages and 4 pairings, and d'Alessio and Rubino rose to 18, Calandriello and d'Alessio to 16 and Petrizzo and Quagliano to 12, while Ferro overtook di Luca for sixteenth; the one before that added 35 marriages and 4 pairings, and d'Amato and Petrizzo rose from 18 marriages to 20; the one before that added 24 marriages and 9 pairings, and Calandriello and d'Amato rose from 25 marriages to 26; the one before that added 45 marriages and 11 pairings, and Calandriello and Petrizzo rose from 26 marriages to 28.)

That's not a coincidence of a couple of prolific individuals — it shows up at every level. Calandriello is also the most common marriage partner for a wide swath of major surnames — di Brizzi, Rubino, Arnone, Pinto, Russo, di Bella, Libretti, Ferro — meaning if a family in Sassano married outside its own name, Calandriello was disproportionately likely to be on the other side of that marriage. d'Amato plays the same role for Feminella, Pellegrino, Trotta, and Florendino. Between them, these two families functioned as something like the connective tissue of the whole town's marriage network — the families everyone else was likely to marry into sooner or later.

Same-surname marriages — two people who already shared a family name marrying each other, which in a town this size usually means some degree of relation even before you trace the exact connection — happened 103 times across the tree. Calandriello leads that list too, with 17, followed by d'Amato at 13 and Petrizzo at 8. For the exact blood relationships behind marriages like these, see the cousin marriage analysis — the two pages are looking at the same underlying pattern from different angles.

Worth noting: almost none of the top surname pairings show a Chicago connection directly — these are overwhelmingly historical, Sassano-based alliances from before the migration, which makes sense given how much deeper the tree's Italian branches run compared to its American ones.
