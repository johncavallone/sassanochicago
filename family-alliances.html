Which families married which.

Cousin marriage told us how closely related couples tended to be. This is a different question: regardless of blood relation, which surnames kept choosing each other, generation after generation?

Two surnames sit at the center of nearly everything: Calandriello and d'Amato. Between them they appear in a huge share of the tree's marriages — Calandriello alone shows up in 516 marriages across 106 different partner surnames, d'Amato in 488 across 117. Calandriello and Petrizzo hold at 26 marriages, still the single most common alliance documented anywhere in the tree, but the gap behind it has widened: Calandriello and d'Amato slip from 25 to 24 after a Calandriello record was removed from the tree, while d'Alessio and Rubino climb from 15 to 17, holding seventh and widening their lead over Calandriello and d'Alessio (now 15). d'Alessio and d'Amato also rise from 12 to 13, which lifts that pairing from eighteenth to thirteenth. The number of marriages counted rose from 6,245 to 6,273 as new families were added. Among the individual surnames, Calandriello holds at 516 marriages across 106 partners, d'Amato reaches 488 across 117, and Petrizzo, third, 412 across 102. d'Alessio adds seven (now 321 across 95 partners) and stays fourth, Trotta and di Brizzi each add a partner surname, and di Bella (238) now has d'Amato rather than Calandriello as its most frequent partner. Same-surname marriages rise by three to 95: d'Amato reaches 9, extending its lead over Petrizzo (8), with Calandriello (15) and di Brizzi (6) unchanged.

That's not a coincidence of a couple of prolific individuals — it shows up at every level. Calandriello is also the most common marriage partner for a wide swath of major surnames — di Brizzi, Rubino, Arnone, Pinto, Russo, di Bella, Libretti, Ferro — meaning if a family in Sassano married outside its own name, Calandriello was disproportionately likely to be on the other side of that marriage. d'Amato plays the same role for Feminella, Pellegrino, Trotta, and Florendino. Between them, these two families functioned as something like the connective tissue of the whole town's marriage network — the families everyone else was likely to marry into sooner or later.

Same-surname marriages — two people who already shared a family name marrying each other, which in a town this size usually means some degree of relation even before you trace the exact connection — happened 95 times across the tree. Calandriello leads that list too, with 15, followed by d'Amato at 9 and Petrizzo at 8. For the exact blood relationships behind marriages like these, see the cousin marriage analysis — the two pages are looking at the same underlying pattern from different angles.

Worth noting: almost none of the top surname pairings show a Chicago connection directly — these are overwhelmingly historical, Sassano-based alliances from before the migration, which makes sense given how much deeper the tree's Italian branches run compared to its American ones.
