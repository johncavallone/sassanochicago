Which families married which.

Cousin marriage told us how closely related couples tended to be. This is a different question: regardless of blood relation, which surnames kept choosing each other, generation after generation?

Two surnames sit at the center of nearly everything: Calandriello and d'Amato. Between them they appear in a huge share of the tree's marriages — Calandriello alone shows up in 541 marriages across 106 different partner surnames, d'Amato in 524 across 119. Calandriello and Petrizzo hold at 28 marriages, still the single most common alliance documented anywhere in the tree, and Calandriello and d'Amato hold at 26 in second. The biggest movers are the d'Alessio families: d'Alessio and Rubino add one to reach 18, climbing from seventh to sixth and drawing level with Calandriello and di Brizzi (18), and Calandriello and d'Alessio add one to reach 16, tying d'Amato and Russo. Petrizzo and Quagliano add one to reach 12 and rise from 26th to 21st. Calandriello and Libretti reach 10, and Calandriello and Quagliano and di Brizzi and Petrizzo reach 9 and 9, but the list is cut at 30 rows and these stay off it. The number of marriages counted rose from 6,515 to 6,546 as new records were added, and the number of distinct surname pairings from 3,249 to 3,253. Among the individual surnames, Calandriello and d'Alessio add five marriages each (541 and 336), Ferro four (211), Rubino and Libretti three (274 and 208) and Petrizzo, Feminella, Trotta, Pinto, di Luca and Stavola two each (439, 322, 307, 237, 210 and 190), while d'Amato, Russo and di Brizzi add one (524, 285 and 256); di Bella, di Miele, Carlo, Pellegrino, Arnone and Florendino hold at 247, 239, 239, 229, 215 and 192. The only change in rank is Ferro overtaking di Luca for sixteenth, reversing last update's swap. Same-surname marriages hold at 102, with Calandriello on 17, d'Amato on 13, Petrizzo on 8 and di Brizzi on 6. (Last update added 35 marriages and 4 pairings, and d'Amato and Petrizzo rose from 18 marriages to 20; the update before that added 24 marriages and 9 pairings, and Calandriello and d'Amato rose from 25 marriages to 26; the one before that added 45 marriages and 11 pairings, and Calandriello and Petrizzo rose from 26 marriages to 28.)

That's not a coincidence of a couple of prolific individuals — it shows up at every level. Calandriello is also the most common marriage partner for a wide swath of major surnames — di Brizzi, Rubino, Arnone, Pinto, Russo, di Bella, Libretti, Ferro — meaning if a family in Sassano married outside its own name, Calandriello was disproportionately likely to be on the other side of that marriage. d'Amato plays the same role for Feminella, Pellegrino, Trotta, and Florendino. Between them, these two families functioned as something like the connective tissue of the whole town's marriage network — the families everyone else was likely to marry into sooner or later.

Same-surname marriages — two people who already shared a family name marrying each other, which in a town this size usually means some degree of relation even before you trace the exact connection — happened 102 times across the tree. Calandriello leads that list too, with 17, followed by d'Amato at 13 and Petrizzo at 8. For the exact blood relationships behind marriages like these, see the cousin marriage analysis — the two pages are looking at the same underlying pattern from different angles.

Worth noting: almost none of the top surname pairings show a Chicago connection directly — these are overwhelmingly historical, Sassano-based alliances from before the migration, which makes sense given how much deeper the tree's Italian branches run compared to its American ones.
