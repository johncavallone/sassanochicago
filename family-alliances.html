Which families married which.

Cousin marriage told us how closely related couples tended to be. This is a different question: regardless of blood relation, which surnames kept choosing each other, generation after generation?

Two surnames sit at the center of nearly everything: Calandriello and d'Amato. Between them they appear in a huge share of the tree's marriages — Calandriello alone shows up in 536 marriages across 106 different partner surnames, d'Amato in 523 across 119. Calandriello and Petrizzo hold at 28 marriages, still the single most common alliance documented anywhere in the tree, and Calandriello and d'Amato hold at 26 in second. The biggest mover is d'Amato and Petrizzo, which add two marriages and rise from 18 to 20, climbing from fifth to fourth past Calandriello and di Brizzi (18). Carlo and d'Amato add one to reach 14, and Petrizzo and Quagliano add one to reach 11 and enter the top thirty, which pushes d'Amato and di Lisa (10) off the bottom of the list. d'Amato and Pinto (now 10), Calandriello and Libretti (9) and di Brizzi and Petrizzo (8) each add a marriage but stay below the list's cutoff. The number of marriages counted rose from 6,480 to 6,515 as new records were added, and the number of distinct surname pairings from 3,245 to 3,249. Among the individual surnames, d'Amato adds seven marriages (523), Petrizzo six (437) and Calandriello, Rubino, di Bella and Pellegrino three each (536, 271, 247 and 229); di Brizzi, di Miele, Carlo, Pinto and di Luca add two each (255, 239, 239, 235 and 208) and d'Alessio, Feminella, Russo, Arnone, Ferro and Libretti one each (331, 320, 284, 215, 207 and 205), while Trotta, Florendino and Stavola hold at 305, 192 and 188. The only change in rank is di Luca overtaking Ferro for sixteenth. Same-surname marriages rise to 102, from 100, with Calandriello on 17, d'Amato on 13, Petrizzo on 8 and di Brizzi on 6. (Last update added 24 marriages and 9 pairings, and Calandriello and d'Amato rose from 25 marriages to 26; the update before that added 45 marriages and 11 pairings, and Calandriello and Petrizzo rose from 26 marriages to 28.)

That's not a coincidence of a couple of prolific individuals — it shows up at every level. Calandriello is also the most common marriage partner for a wide swath of major surnames — di Brizzi, Rubino, Arnone, Pinto, Russo, di Bella, Libretti, Ferro — meaning if a family in Sassano married outside its own name, Calandriello was disproportionately likely to be on the other side of that marriage. d'Amato plays the same role for Feminella, Pellegrino, Trotta, and Florendino. Between them, these two families functioned as something like the connective tissue of the whole town's marriage network — the families everyone else was likely to marry into sooner or later.

Same-surname marriages — two people who already shared a family name marrying each other, which in a town this size usually means some degree of relation even before you trace the exact connection — happened 102 times across the tree. Calandriello leads that list too, with 17, followed by d'Amato at 13 and Petrizzo at 8. For the exact blood relationships behind marriages like these, see the cousin marriage analysis — the two pages are looking at the same underlying pattern from different angles.

Worth noting: almost none of the top surname pairings show a Chicago connection directly — these are overwhelmingly historical, Sassano-based alliances from before the migration, which makes sense given how much deeper the tree's Italian branches run compared to its American ones.
