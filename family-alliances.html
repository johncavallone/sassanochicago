Which families married which.

Two surnames sit at the center of nearly everything: Calandriello and d'Amato. Between them they appear in a huge share of the tree's marriages — Calandriello alone shows up in 544 marriages across 106 different partner surnames, d'Amato in 525 across 119. Calandriello and Petrizzo hold at 28 marriages, still the single most common alliance documented anywhere in the tree, and Calandriello and d'Amato hold at 26 in second. This update added 32 marriages and 13 pairings. The top of the list holds: Calandriello and Petrizzo stay at 28, Calandriello and d'Amato at 26 and Calandriello and Rubino at 22. The movement is further down: d'Alessio and di Brizzi, and Calandriello and Zozzaro, each add one to reach 12, which lifts them into the group on 12 and leaves just three pairs on 11 in the table. Feminella and Pellegrino reach 6 and Feminella is the biggest gainer among individual surnames, adding four marriages (to 326); Calandriello adds three (544), Rubino, di Brizzi and Pellegrino two each (276, 258 and 231), and d'Amato, Petrizzo, d'Alessio, Trotta, di Miele, Carlo, Pinto, Ferro, di Luca and Stavola one each (525, 440, 337, 308, 240, 240, 238, 212, 211 and 191); Russo, di Bella, Arnone, Libretti and Florendino hold at 285, 247, 215, 208 and 192. No surname changes rank, and the same-surname count holds at 102, with Calandriello on 17, d'Amato on 13, Petrizzo on 8 and di Brizzi on 6. Trotta, Rubino and Carlo each gain a new partner surname (89, 78 and 84). (Last update added 31 marriages and 4 pairings, and d'Alessio and Rubino rose to 18, Calandriello and d'Alessio to 16 and Petrizzo and Quagliano to 12, while Ferro overtook di Luca for sixteenth; the update before that added 35 marriages and 4 pairings, and d'Amato and Petrizzo rose from 18 marriages to 20; the one before that added 24 marriages and 9 pairings, and Calandriello and d'Amato rose from 25 marriages to 26; the one before that added 45 marriages and 11 pairings, and Calandriello and Petrizzo rose from 26 marriages to 28.)

That's not a coincidence of a couple of prolific individuals — it shows up at every level. Calandriello is also the most common marriage partner for a wide swath of major surnames — di Brizzi, Rubino, Arnone, Pinto, Russo, di Bella, Libretti, Ferro — meaning if a family in Sassano married outside its own name, Calandriello was disproportionately likely to be on the other side of that marriage. d'Amato plays the same role for Feminella, Pellegrino, Trotta, and Florendino. Between them, these two families functioned as something like the connective tissue of the whole town's marriage network — the families everyone else was likely to marry into sooner or later.

Same-surname marriages — two people who already shared a family name marrying each other, which in a town this size usually means some degree of relation even before you trace the exact connection — happened 102 times across the tree. Calandriello leads that list too, with 17, followed by d'Amato at 13 and Petrizzo at 8. For the exact blood relationships behind marriages like these, see the cousin marriage analysis — the two pages are looking at the same underlying pattern from different angles.

Worth noting: almost none of the top surname pairings show a Chicago connection directly — these are overwhelmingly historical, Sassano-based alliances from before the migration, which makes sense given how much deeper the tree's Italian branches run compared to its American ones.
