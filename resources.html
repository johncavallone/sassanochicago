The full research toolkit

Resources.

Every source, database, and archive behind this project — trees, parish record images, plot maps, cemetery indexes, and more.

Trees & Indexes

  • The Sassano ProjectPeter Barbella's genealogy database index and historical content. San Giovanni Evangelista baptism, marriage, and death indexes from 1644–1910. Sassano civil records of births, marriages, and deaths from 1809–1910.
  • Monte San Giacomo ProjectSimilar to the Sassano Project — residents of this neighboring town often married Sassanesi. Worth checking if a family you're researching suddenly "disappears." Common surnames: Amato, Calabrese, Caporrino, Chirichella, di Lisa, Monaco, Pantoliano, Romano, Spina, Totaro.
  • Valle di DianoPeter Barbella's documentation of the ancestors of Sassano and surrounding towns — a continual work in progress, currently 14,000+ individuals.
  • Sassano–Chicago Pedigree TreeMy online tree of 18,800+ individuals via RootsMagic — an alternative to requesting direct access to my Ancestry tree. Use alongside Peter Barbella's Valle di Diano data.
  • Ancestry TreeAn invitation to my Ancestry.com family tree, including more than 18,800 Sassano ancestors. If you have Sassano roots, there's an extremely high probability we're related — email cavallonegenealogy@gmail.com for access.
  • PDF of Sassano IndividualsAs of May 26, 2021 — 14,294 individuals either born, married, or died in Sassano, with birth, parents, marriage date, and spouse. Searchable. Best used alongside the Sassano Project spreadsheets, especially for multiple weddings.

Books

  • From Sassano to 48th & Federal Street: An Italian Migration to America at the Turn of the 20th Century
  • Reconstructing Italians in Chicago10th Anniversary 2021 Edition — Casa Italia includes John Cavallone's selection "From Sassano to Armour Ave."
  • Social Conditions Among the Negroes on Federal Street Between Forty-Fifth Street and Fifty-Third StreetAlice Quan Rood, 1924. Focused on the African American community, but the Sassanesi lived on this same stretch of Federal Street at the same time — Rood mentions the Italians directly. An unflinching look at what our ancestors and the African American community lived through, side by side.
  • 1800 Kilometers In A FIAT 500The Sassano Project's founder, Peter Barbella, and his wife Gayann document their 2014 genealogy quest in Italy. Worth reading — genuinely funny.
